# Montana: 2026 general election research

Researched 2026-10-23 for the November 3, 2026 election.

## Sources

**Official**

- Secretary of State (sosmt.gov): **unreadable**. Every request got a firewall 403, through both curl and WebFetch.
- Instead, the SOS "Proposed 2026 Ballot Issues" page was read as the Wayback Machine archived it on 2026-10-10.
  It lists CI-132, CI-133 and I-194 as "Qualified for 2026 General Election ballot (8/17/2026)", and no
  legislative referral appears.
- MCA 13-27-105, read on archive.legmt.gov, gives the effective-date rules for ballot issues.

**Secondary (relied on because SOS is blocked)**

- Montana Free Press 2026 Election Guide (updated Oct 22) for candidates and name spellings, plus MTFP's Oct 13
  initiatives guide (three statewide measures).
- Ballotpedia pages for the Senate, House, Supreme Court and the three measures, including the ballot text.

The two secondary sources agree on every printed candidate.

## Findings

- U.S. Senate (open seat, after Daines withdrew in March) has 4 candidates:
  - Alme (R)
  - Bankhead (D)
  - Austin (L)
  - Bodnar (Independent)
- Supreme Court Seat 4 (Baker not running) is nonpartisan: Amy Eddy and Dan Wilson.
- Public Service Commission races are by district, so they are left out.
- U.S. House:
  - MT-1: Flint (R), Forstag (D) and Sheedy (L). Incumbent Zinke is not on the ballot.
  - MT-2: Downing (R), Miller (D), McCracken (L) and Eisenhauer (Independent).
- Measures: 3 in total.
  - CI-132 keeps judicial elections nonpartisan.
  - CI-133 creates a constitutional right to initiative and referendum.
  - I-194 is a statute barring political spending by "artificial persons".
  - A Butte data-center initiative is local and is left out.
- When approved measures take effect:
  - Constitutional initiatives take effect July 1 after approval (2027-07-01, the representative date).
  - I-194 reportedly sets January 1, 2027.
- Polls close at 8 PM MST, which is 2026-11-04T03:00Z.

## Gaps / unconfirmed

- No candidate or ballot statement was confirmed against an official SOS document.
- Jami Dee Woodman (Senate) is left out. Ballotpedia calls her a write-in, while MTFP lists her as
  "no party affiliation".
- The ballot-face party wording, the Supreme Court office label, and any I-194 fiscal statement were not checked.
- It was not checked whether CI-132 or CI-133 sets its own effective date.
