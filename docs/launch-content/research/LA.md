# Louisiana: November 3, 2026 election research

## What kind of election Nov 3 is
Mixed. The SOS calendar calls it "U.S. Senate General/Open U.S. Representative Primary/Open Primary Election".
- **U.S. Senate: general election.** The new closed party primaries were held on May 16, with runoffs on June 27. The nominees are Julia Letlow (R), who beat incumbent Bill Cassidy, and Jamie Davis (D). The winner on Nov 3 is elected.
- **U.S. House: open (jungle) primary.** After *Louisiana v. Callais* (April 29, 2026), the May House primaries were suspended, and Act 7 of 2026 made the House an open primary for 2026. All parties run on one ballot. If no one wins a majority, the top two meet in a runoff on **December 12, 2026**.
- **Map:** Act 2 of 2026 (SB 121), signed May 29. It has 6 districts, expected 5R/1D. It drops the second majority-Black district, and the 5th and 6th are open seats.

## Results
- **1 statewide race:** U.S. Senator, 2 candidates. District races (Supreme Court, PSC, BESE) are left out.
- **6 House districts, 39 candidates:** 4, 5, 6, 5, 9 and 10 per district.
- **10 constitutional amendments:** Amendments 1 to 10 (property tax, bail, governor term limit, retirement debt, water lines, expropriation).
- **Polls:** 6 AM to 8 PM CST, statewide. Close is 02:00 UTC on Nov 4.
- **Effective date:** 20 days after the governor's proclamation (Art. XIII §1(C)), unless the amendment says otherwise. 2026-12-10 is an estimate. Amendments 1, 2, 9 and 10 say Jan 1, 2027, and Amendment 6 says Jan 1, 2028.

## Gaps
- **Map lawsuits:** suits against Act 2 were filed, and their current status was not checked.
- **Act 7 text:** not read. The open-primary rule comes from SOS pages.
- **Amendment glyph fixes:** "ef fective" was rejoined and the apostrophe in "year's" was inferred. No sample ballot was checked.
- **Party labels:** the printed ballot labels (possibly abbreviations) were not seen. The SOS's "Democrat" is written as "Democratic".
