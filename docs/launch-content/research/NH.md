# New Hampshire: 2026 general election research

Researched 2026-10-23 for the November 3, 2026 election.

## Sources

**Official**

- NH General Court, CACR 13 (the version adopted by both bodies), which gives the official question wording (sec. V) and the effective-date rule (sec. VII):
  https://gc.nh.gov/bill_status/legacy/bs2016/billText.aspx?id=1803&txtFormat=html&sy=2026
- RSA 659:4 (polling hours): https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/659/659-4.htm

The NH Secretary of State site (sos.nh.gov and its documents) returned HTTP 403 to both WebFetch and curl. That means the official candidate list, the primary results and the sample ballots could not be read. Web search was also unavailable this session.

**Secondary (used for candidates)**

- Ballotpedia pages for the Senate, Governor, U.S. House and 2026 ballot measures.
- Wikipedia pages for the Senate, Governor and House races (wikitext, including the primary results).

## Findings

- **U.S. Senate (open seat):** Chris Pappas (D), John E. Sununu (R), Edmond LaPlante (Constitution).
- **Governor:** Kelly Ayotte (R, incumbent), Cinde Warmington (D), Stephen Villee (Libertarian).
- **NH-1:** Stefany Shaheen (D), Anthony DiLorenzo (R).
- **NH-2:** Maggie Goodlander (D, incumbent), Lily Tang Williams (R), Robbie Mahrou (Independent).
- **Primary (Sep 8):** Pappas beat Manzur. Sununu beat Scott Brown. Shaheen narrowly beat Maura Sullivan (36.9% to 34.7%). DiLorenzo won with 51.5% and Williams with 64.9%.
- **Question 1:** the legislature's CACR 13 would eliminate the register of probate office. It needs a two-thirds vote; a similar question failed in 2022 with 62.9%. It is the only statewide measure.
- **Effective date:** the amendment takes effect when the governor proclaims its adoption. 2026-12-02 is used as an estimated representative date.
- **Polls:** towns set their own hours (open by 11 AM, close no earlier than 7 PM). The latest close is 8 PM EST, which is 2026-11-04T01:00Z.

## Disagreements

- Ballotpedia and Wikipedia list the same candidates and parties. They differ only in how names are written: "Edmond Laplante" vs "Edmond J. LaPlante Jr.", and "Lily Williams" vs "Lily Tang Williams".

## Gaps

- The names and party labels exactly as printed on the ballot are not confirmed against the SOS list or a sample ballot. This includes whether Mahrou and Villee are on the printed ballot.
- The "Question 1" numbering is assumed.
- The proclamation date is an estimate.
- No official per-town list of polling hours was read.
