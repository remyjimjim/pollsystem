# Managing polls

**Manage Polls** lists every poll that covers any part of your area.

## Finding polls

Filter by **Poll type**, **Title contains**, **State**, **County**,
**Zipcode**, or words in the poll's **Notes**, then click **Search**. Disabled
polls are hidden unless you tick **Show disabled**, and polls that cover the
whole country are hidden unless you tick **Show nationwide**. Each row shows the
poll's title, type, creator, area (**Purview**) and closing date.

## Disabling a poll

Untick a poll's **Enabled?** box, then choose where to stop it:

- **This zipcode only**
- **The whole county**
- **The whole state**
- **Everywhere (whole poll)**

You can only choose places inside your area. A disabled poll disappears from
search and can't be answered **in that place**; elsewhere it carries on. The
dialog lists any **Active blocks** already on the poll, and **Remove** lifts one.

Tick **Enabled?** again to lift every block you're able to lift.

## Notes

The **Notes** column shows the start of a poll's latest note, or **Add note**.
Click it to open the poll's notes:

1. Click **Add note** and write your note (up to 2000 characters), for example
   why you disabled the poll.
2. Tick **Email a copy to …** to send it to the poll's creator too.
3. Click **Save**, or **Cancel** to drop it.

Below that are all the poll's notes, newest first. A note you save goes to the
top.

## Changing a poll's title, summary, status or close date

**Edit** lets you change a poll's **Title**, its **Summary** (questionnaires and
ballot measures), its **Status** (Published, Closed or Archived) and its **Close
date**. Changing an election's close date gives
all its ballot measures the same close date. A **Reason** is required. It's saved as a note on the
poll, and you can email it to the creator.

You can edit polls inside your area only.

## History

**History** on a poll's row shows its history: published, reopened, closed,
archived, restored, switched off or back on, and edited, with when, by whom
(with their email) and details. You see the history of polls in your area, and
your own polls.
