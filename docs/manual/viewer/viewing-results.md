# Viewing results

Anyone can view results, signed in or not. To open them:

- In [Finding polls](finding-polls.md), click **View results** on the poll's row.
- After you vote, you're taken to the results automatically.

Under the poll's title, **Purview area** says where the poll is for:
**Nationwide**, or the states, counties or zipcodes it covers (the first few,
then how many more). The page also shows how many people have responded.

## What you'll see

Results are shown as bars with a **percentage**, rounded to a whole number.

- **Questionnaires:** each question, with the share of people who picked each
  answer.
- **Ballot measures:** the share who voted **Yes** and the share who voted
  **No**.
- **Elections:** each office, and under it each candidate. **Yes** is the share
  of voters who chose that candidate. **No** is the share who voted for that
  office but chose someone else.
- **Ranked-choice races:** the count **round by round**. Each round shows
  every remaining candidate's share of the ballots still counting. If no one
  has more than half, the last-place candidate is **eliminated**, and their
  ballots count for each voter's next choice. With two left, the one ahead
  is the **Winner**. Ballots with no choices left are listed under each
  round. Ties for last place go to whoever did better in earlier rounds,
  then alphabetical order.

## District maps

Every state's 2026 midterm results pages link to its **district map**, for
example **Washington district map** (`/maps/WA`). States with one House seat
(Alaska, Delaware, North Dakota, South Dakota, Vermont, Wyoming) show the
whole state as one district; in Alaska's ranked-choice race the map counts
1st choices. States that redrew their districts for 2026 use their new
official maps: Alabama, California, Florida, Louisiana, North Carolina,
Ohio, Texas and Utah. **Tennessee** shows **numbered boxes** in place of the
map until its new official map is available. Each congressional district
shows its U.S. House race:

- **top 2** (the default): the two **parties** with the most votes, as
  *X% / Y%*, each in its party's colour. A party's candidates are added
  together, so two Democrats count as one Democratic share.
- **Dem**, **Rep**, **Ind** or **Other**: that party's share in each
  district. Ind covers independents, "No party listed" and non-partisan
  candidates. Other covers every other party, such as Pacific Green or
  Libertarian, in orange.

Change any colour with its colour box; your choices are remembered on this
device. Pick a **District** to zoom in and see all its candidates, or tap a
district on the map. Pick a **Ballot measure** to see Yes / No among each
district ballot's voters, with the leading answer in bold.

The map follows the same privacy rules as results: **—** means too few
people have answered there so far. It's an informal poll of BallotBox
members, not official results or a forecast.

## Results protect people's privacy

- **No results until a poll has its minimum number of respondents.** While
  BallotBox is getting started the minimum is **1**, so results show from the
  first response. Once **100 people** have voted it becomes **4**: until a poll
  has 4, the page says how many have responded so far, because with fewer
  people a result could show how particular people voted.
- **Results update in steps of that minimum.** At 4, the page shows the
  results as they were when the 4th, 8th, 12th… person responded, so a single
  new vote, or a single changed answer, never changes what you see on its own.
  At 1, while BallotBox is getting started, every response shows straight
  away.
- **Percentages only.** Exact counts aren't shown.

## Comments

Under a question, candidate or measure, **N public comments** opens the
comments people chose to make public. They're anonymous, and follow the same
rules as the results: none until the poll has its minimum number of
respondents, and new ones appear with each step. If you narrow the results by place, only comments from
people in that group show.

## Inside or outside the poll's area

Every poll covers an area (its *purview*): a zipcode, a county, a state, or the
whole country. Anyone may respond, so results can include people from elsewhere.
Two checkboxes split the respondents by where they live:

- **Show responses from within the poll's purview**: people living inside the
  poll's area.
- **Show responses from outside the poll's purview**: everyone else.

Both are ticked to start with, so you see everyone. Untick one to see only the
other group. Results update as soon as you click.

The number in brackets after each is how many respondents are in that group,
e.g. "(32)". Like the results, it updates in steps, not with every vote. If
either group is too small to show without narrowing down who's in it, both say
**"(fewer than 4)"**.

A ballot measure covers the same area as the election it belongs to.

## Narrowing by place

You can also look at the respondents from a particular place:

1. Pick one or more **States**.
2. Optionally narrow to **Counties**, then **Zipcodes**.
3. Press **Apply**. **Clear** removes the place filter.

The line above the results shows how many respondents are counted, and which
filter is applied.

## Why are the results hidden?

You may see "Not enough responses in this group to display (privacy
protection)". It appears when you've narrowed the results (by place, or by
unticking one of the two boxes) to a group of **fewer than 4 people**. With so
few people in a group, the results could reveal how particular people voted.

To see results again, widen your choice: pick a larger area, **Clear** the place
filter, or tick both boxes.

If the page says how many people have responded so far but shows no results,
the poll hasn't reached the minimum yet (4, once BallotBox has 100 voters).

People who haven't entered a zipcode yet can't be placed, so they appear only in
the full, unfiltered results.
